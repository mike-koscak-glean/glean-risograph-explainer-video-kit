# Shape the Risograph Video Story

Produce an approved story before visual design or production begins.

## Workflow

1. Read `project/brief.md`, `project/facts-and-claims.md`, and all approved source material. For workplace information, use Glean tools rather than guessing.
2. Identify the audience, decision or learning outcome, current tension, core idea, evidence limits, desired duration, and closing action.
3. Ask one concise round of questions only for gaps that materially change the story.
4. Draft two or three possible narrative angles when the best framing is not obvious. Explain the tradeoff in one sentence each.
5. Recommend one angle and draft `project/story.md` using the required output contract below.
6. Review every customer fact, system, date, metric, and value claim against `project/facts-and-claims.md`. Add missing entries rather than silently assuming them.
7. Present the story for approval. Do not begin storyboard design, source editing, ElevenLabs generation, or rendering.
8. After explicit approval, update the story gate and change log in `project/STATUS.md`.

## Story output contract

`project/story.md` must contain:

- Working title and one-sentence promise
- Audience and job to be done
- Core tension
- Narrative thesis
- Ordered sections, each with purpose and takeaway
- Concrete examples or use cases
- Evidence status and unresolved questions
- Proposed opening and closing
- Duration and density recommendation
- Content intentionally excluded

## Quality bar

- The opening establishes why the video matters within 15 seconds.
- The story is understandable without detailed product knowledge.
- Each section advances one argument rather than listing features.
- General videos teach a durable concept; customer videos stay grounded in approved context.
- The story can be visualized, but this phase does not prescribe final scenes.
