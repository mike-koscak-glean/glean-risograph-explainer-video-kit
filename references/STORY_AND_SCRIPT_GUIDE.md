# Story and Script Guide

A strong engine cannot rescue an unclear story. Start with the job of the video and the audience decision.

## Recommended length

- General concept: 3–4 minutes.
- Customer or pilot story: 4–6 minutes.
- Aim for roughly 145–175 spoken words per minute after delivery cues and pauses.
- Keep each narration line focused on one visual beat, often 15–35 words.

## General explainer structure

1. **Opening tension:** Ask the question or show the fragmentation.
2. **Current state:** Explain why the problem persists.
3. **Model:** Show how Glean connects, indexes, understands, and respects permissions.
4. **Concrete example:** Follow one realistic question through the system.
5. **Broader implication:** Explain what the shared foundation enables.
6. **Close:** Return to the opening idea with one memorable line.

## Customer video structure

1. Customer journey or operating context.
2. Current friction across teams and tools.
3. How Glean provides the common foundation.
4. Two to four agreed or proposed use-case stories.
5. Agents or workflow extension when relevant.
6. Value framed against customer baselines.
7. Proposed pilot or next proof step.
8. Close without unstable dates or unsupported commitments.

## Writing narration

- Write for the ear, not a slide.
- Prefer contractions and direct verbs.
- Use short setup lines followed by a clear payoff.
- Name systems only when they help the audience understand the workflow.
- Let the visual carry lists where possible.
- Avoid repeating the exact headline word-for-word unless emphasis is useful.
- End each scene with a thought that naturally opens the next one.

Delivery cues such as `[warmly]`, `[curious]`, and `[confident]` can guide supported ElevenLabs models. Keep them sparse. The clean script text must remain available for captions and transcript comparison.

## Evidence discipline

Every claim is one of:

- **Confirmed:** supported by an approved source.
- **Proposed:** planned but not final.
- **Illustrative:** fictional or directional example.
- **Unknown:** missing or blocked evidence.

Do not turn proposed thresholds, directional ROI assumptions, or self-reported figures into achieved results. When quality is difficult to measure, show observable outputs or owner review instead of arbitrary percentages.

## Script approval checklist

- Does the first 15 seconds establish why the video matters?
- Can a new audience follow it without reading every label?
- Is each beat visualizable?
- Does the script use the audience’s language?
- Are dates, metrics, systems, and customer facts stable?
- Is the close specific enough to remember but safe enough to share?
